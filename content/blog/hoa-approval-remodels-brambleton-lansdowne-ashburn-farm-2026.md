A remodel can satisfy a county permit requirement and still need an association decision. Treat those as separate items in your planning file, with the relevant drawings, application versions, and written outcomes kept together.

For homeowners in [Brambleton](/service-areas/brambleton-va), [Lansdowne](/service-areas/lansdowne-va), and Ashburn Farm in the [Ashburn area](/service-areas/ashburn-va), the first step is identifying the association that governs the specific property. A community name or postal address is not enough to establish the applicable rules.

The official sources below were checked September 27, 2026. Review calendars and documents can change, so confirm the current requirements before submitting.

## Brambleton: use the published review calendar

[Brambleton Community Association's Design Review page](https://brambletonhoa.com/252/Design-Review) says exterior appearance changes need Covenants Committee review, including color or material changes and removals.

The committee normally meets on the second Monday of the month. Its stated deadline is 9:00 a.m. on the Friday ten days before the meeting, with written decisions sent five to seven business days afterward. The association can change the calendar; verify the date for your submission.

That page links the application, checklist, guidelines, and sample plats. Use those documents to assemble the packet and ask the association to clarify anything uncertain before work begins.

Do not use the normal meeting cycle as a promised approval date. An incomplete packet or requested revision may require another step. Keep purchasing and proposed construction dates flexible until the written decision and other required approvals are in hand.

## Ashburn Farm: exterior approval is a real requirement

[Ashburn Farm Association's resale guidance](https://www.ashburnfarmassociation.org/resalepacket) states that exterior modifications require an Exterior Alteration Application and association approval. It also explains that resale inspections check whether the work matches the approved application.

Request the current design standards and submission instructions from the association. Confirm whether your project needs neighbor notice and whether an existing alteration has a recorded approval. Do not mistake the resale-processing timetable or resale fees on that page for the architectural-review timetable or application fee.

A photo of a similar improvement elsewhere in the community may help communicate your design idea. It does not replace the current rules for your lot or prove that the other improvement was approved.

## Lansdowne: identify the right association first

There is more than one association in the Lansdowne area. [Lansdowne on the Potomac HOA](https://lansdownehoa.com/) provides resident resources, while [Lansdowne Village Greens](https://lvghoa.frontsteps.com/public/folders/Yet5rrWbtnDGDcqE5o7zaeAssMtfj3Dqswrr) has its own document portal and design-review application.

Confirm which organization governs your address and whether any additional association or condominium rules apply. This guide does not assign a shared Lansdowne fee, approved color palette, or turnaround time. Obtain those answers from the association responsible for your property.

## Build a packet that describes the proposed work

Use the association's actual checklist as the controlling list. A useful preparation file includes:

1. The current application and the owner's property details.
2. A drawing locating the proposed improvement on the lot.
3. Dimensions and exterior views that make the shape understandable.
4. Product names, finishes, colors, and samples if requested.
5. Existing-condition photographs and a clear description of anything being removed.
6. Neighbor acknowledgements or notices where the association requires them.
7. A record of questions, revisions, and the final written decision.

Keep the version submitted to the county consistent with the version submitted to the association. If one reviewer requires a change, check whether the other application or the contractor's estimate also needs revision.

## County permits still follow the project

Use Loudoun's official pages for [decks](https://www.loudoun.gov/1166/Decks), [finished basements](https://www.loudoun.gov/1172/Finished-Basements), and [additions and alterations](https://www.loudoun.gov/5387/Residential-Additions-and-Alterations) to identify the county application. Confirm the parcel's jurisdiction and any town zoning requirement with the relevant office.

Interior-only work may avoid exterior design review, but do not assume an exemption from all association or condominium requirements. Ask about your scope. A window, vent, addition, or basement escape opening can change the exterior even when the project begins as an interior remodel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I order materials when the county permit is ready?

Check every required approval first. A county permit does not tell you whether the association accepts a particular exterior product or color. Ask how substitutions would be reviewed before making non-returnable purchases.

### Does an interior kitchen or bath need HOA approval?

Confirm with the association rather than relying on a blanket rule. Describe any exterior openings or vent changes, and ask about restrictions specific to your property type.

### Who should keep the approval records?

Keep your own copies of the application, approved drawings, conditions, and final decisions. Share the relevant versions with each provider so the work is priced and built from the same scope.
