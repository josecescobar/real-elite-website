The question "how long will my kitchen take?" has two answers. One is how long the room is torn up. The other is how long it takes from the first conversation to cooking again. The second number is the one that runs your life, and most of it is decided before anyone picks up a pry bar.

This guide covers kitchens in [Ashburn](/service-areas/ashburn-va), [Leesburg](/service-areas/leesburg-va), and [Brambleton](/service-areas/brambleton-va). It uses published estimates and county and town sources checked September 27, 2026. We have not published our own durations, so every number below names who said it. For the budget side, see the [Loudoun kitchen remodel cost guide](/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost-loudoun-county-2026).

## Three kitchens, three schedules

A finish refresh, a layout change, and a kitchen that needs more floor area are different projects. Comparing their timelines tells you as little as comparing their prices.

- **Finish refresh:** new cabinets, counters, backsplash, and fixtures in the same places. The plumbing and walls stay put.
- **Layout change:** the sink, range, or island moves, or a wall comes out. That adds trade permits, possibly structural plans, and rough-in inspections.
- **More room:** a bump-out or addition. That is a different project with its own approvals. Our [Loudoun addition cost guide](/blog/home-addition-cost-loudoun-county-2026) covers it.

[Denny + Gardner's kitchen timeline article](https://www.dennyandgardner.com/blog/kitchen-remodel-timeline-virginia-and-dc) (September 2022) draws the same line. It gives about 6 to 8 weeks for construction when you replace components in place, and 10 to 12 weeks for structural changes and a reconfigured layout. It adds about 4 to 8 weeks of planning and design beforehand.

## What published timelines say

[EA Home Design's 2026 Northern Virginia kitchen guide](https://eahomedesign.com/how-much-does-a-kitchen-remodel-cost-in-northern-virginia-2026-price-guide/) (June 13, 2026) says a typical full kitchen remodel takes 6 to 12 weeks from demolition to completion. It breaks the job into these phases:

- **Design and planning:** 2 to 6 weeks
- **Permitting:** 1 to 4 weeks
- **Demolition:** 2 to 4 days
- **Plumbing and electrical rough-in:** 1 to 2 weeks
- **Cabinets and countertops:** 2 to 4 weeks
- **Flooring, backsplash, and finishes:** 1 to 2 weeks
- **Final inspection and punch list:** a few days

[Fixr's kitchen timeline article](https://www.fixr.com/articles/how-long-does-a-kitchen-remodel-take) (January 2025) gives 6 to 12 weeks for an average remodel. It says a project with load-bearing walls, moved water or drain lines, or custom cabinets is more like 3 to 8 months from start to finish.

These are other firms' estimates for their own scopes. They are useful for seeing which phases take time. They are not a promise for your kitchen or ours.

## Decide before demolition

Every published timeline agrees on one thing: the delays come from decisions and orders, not from demolition. Settle these before the kitchen comes apart.

**A measured plan.** Field measurements, the appliance list, and every change to a sink, range, doorway, or wall go on one drawing. Cabinets are ordered from that drawing, so it has to be right.

**Appliance models.** List the actual models, even if you are buying them yourself. Their dimensions, venting, and power requirements set the cabinet sizes. EA notes that special-order materials are among the most common causes of delay.

**Cabinet construction.** This is the biggest schedule choice in the room. Fixr's published lead times:

- **Stock:** one to two weeks for delivery, then two to three days to install
- **Semi-custom:** four to eight weeks for delivery, plus two to three days to install
- **Custom:** three to six months or longer to build and deliver, plus a few days to install

If you want custom cabinetry, order it early. The practical order is design, then order the cabinets, then schedule demolition around their delivery date. That keeps the kitchen from sitting gutted while you wait.

**Counters.** Stone is templated after the base cabinets are installed, because the fabricator measures the real boxes. [Cambria](https://www.cambriausa.com/cambria-style/blog/cambria-quartz-countertop-installation) says fabrication takes approximately two weeks after templating, depending on the size of the job and slab shipping, and installation takes two to five hours. Plan to be without counters in that gap.

## Permits: what triggers them

Virginia's building code [exempts some ordinary repairs from permits](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title13/agency5/chapter63/section80/). Among them: replacing cabinetry or trim, replacing floor finishes, painting, and replacing plumbing fixtures without altering the supply, drain, or vent piping. The same section requires a permit to remove or add a wall, or to change water supply or electrical wiring. Countertops are not named in the exemptions. Ask the building office before you assume a counter-and-cabinet swap needs nothing.

For a layout change, Loudoun's [Residential Additions and Alterations page](https://www.loudoun.gov/5387/Residential-Additions-and-Alterations) sets the route:

- **Fee:** 1% of construction cost, plus $130 for plan review
- **Plans:** all structural elements detailed on a complete set of plans
- **Trade permits:** electrical, gas, mechanical, and plumbing, each separate. Gas permits for residential alterations need plans review as of October 1, 2025.

The county's [residential permits dashboard](https://www.loudoun.gov/6084/Residential-Permits-Processing-Dashboard) lists a 15-day building review target and a 10-day zoning target for alterations, and 2 days for trade permits. It doesn't say whether those are business or calendar days. Intake before review can take up to ten business days. A review target is not a start date. It doesn't include design, ordering, or any revisions the reviewer asks for.

Real Elite does not take electrical work. A separately licensed electrician carries that permit, and their schedule belongs in the plan. For the full county path, see our [Loudoun permits and HOA guide](/blog/loudoun-county-permits-hoa-guide-2026).

## Leesburg: town zoning first

For property inside Leesburg's town limits, the county requires an approved town zoning permit with the building application. The Town of Leesburg's [community development FAQ](https://www.leesburgva.gov/departments/community-development/community-development-faqs) says a town zoning permit is required for almost any construction in town, including interior alterations. For a kitchen remodel, the town asks for a drawing of the new improvements. It does not have to be professionally prepared. Loudoun County then reviews the building code on the town's behalf. Homes in the town's historic districts may also need Board of Architectural Review approval.

Many homes with a Leesburg mailing address, including [Lansdowne](/service-areas/lansdowne-va), are outside town limits. Check the parcel's jurisdiction first.

## HOA review only when the outside changes

An interior kitchen usually doesn't need association approval. A new window, a moved exterior door, or a new range-hood vent through the siding changes the outside, and that changes the answer. See our [HOA approval guide for Brambleton, Lansdowne, and Ashburn Farm](/blog/hoa-approval-remodels-brambleton-lansdowne-ashburn-farm-2026), and build the association's review calendar into the schedule before you order anything for the exterior.

## Living in the house without a kitchen

Plan for the weeks when the room is unusable:

- **A temporary kitchen:** the refrigerator, a microwave, and a coffee maker in the dining room or garage.
- **A sink:** a laundry or bar sink for washing up, since the kitchen sink will be gone for a stretch.
- **Dust and access:** agree which doors the crew uses, where materials are stored, and how the rest of the house is protected.
- **Deliveries:** cabinets arrive in many boxes. Decide in advance where they will be stored and inspected.

## A planning sequence, not a promise

For a layout-change kitchen, the order usually runs like this. How long each step takes depends on your selections and approvals.

1. Walkthrough, measurements, and a written scope
2. Design, selections, and the appliance list
3. Cabinet order placed, with a confirmed delivery date
4. Permit applications, and town zoning first in Leesburg
5. Demolition, scheduled against the cabinet delivery
6. Plumbing, mechanical, and electrical rough-in, then inspections
7. Walls closed, cabinets installed
8. Countertop template, fabrication, and installation
9. Backsplash, fixtures, appliances, and final inspections

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the biggest thing that delays a kitchen?

In the published sources, cabinets and special-order materials. Choosing cabinets and appliances before demolition does the most for the schedule.

### Can the permit run while the cabinets are being built?

Often, yes. The plan, permit review, and cabinet production can overlap, as long as the layout is final before the cabinets are ordered.

### Should I start before the holidays?

Work backward from your cabinet delivery date, not forward from a start date. If the cabinets can't arrive in time, move demolition rather than live without a kitchen through the holidays.
