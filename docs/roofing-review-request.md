# Roofing review requests — what to send and why

**Goal:** 3–5 Google reviews from roofing customers in Martinsburg and Charles Town that use the word *roof* and name the town.

## Why this specific ask

Your profile has 6 reviews. Four are decks, one a fence, one stone veneer. Google's derived topics for the business read **`deck (4)`, `wood (2)`, `deck replacement (2)`** — there is no roofing signal at all.

A 5×5 rank grid for "roof replacement" across 8 miles around Martinsburg found the business in **4 of 25 points, and in the local 3-pack at none of them**. The four hits are all on the eastern edge, at points where only 4–11 businesses appear at all. Everywhere competition is real — 15 to 21 results — the listing is absent.

Reviews are the strongest lever on that. A deck review does not help a roofing query.

Two of the six reviews are also from **Springfield VA** and one from **Winchester VA**. Good reviews, wrong geography for the Eastern Panhandle.

## Who to ask

Roofing customers from the last 6–9 months, in this order:

1. Martinsburg / Berkeley County
2. Charles Town / Ranson / Jefferson County
3. Inwood, Hedgesville, Spring Mills

Storm or insurance jobs are especially worth asking — that language matches how people search.

## The message

Short, specific, no script feel. Text is better than email.

> Hey [Name] — Jose with Real Elite. Hope the roof's been holding up well through the rain we've had.
>
> Quick favor if you've got a minute: we're trying to get found by more folks in [Martinsburg] who need a roof, and Google reviews are pretty much the whole game for that. If you'd leave us a quick one, it genuinely helps.
>
> [link]
>
> No pressure at all, and thanks either way — it was a good job to be on.

### Why it's worded that way

- **"who need a roof" / "[Martinsburg]"** — plants the two words you need without telling anyone what to write. People echo the framing naturally.
- **"Google reviews are pretty much the whole game"** — true, and it gives a reason. People help when they understand why.
- **No star rating mentioned.** Asking for "a 5-star review" is against Google's policy and reads badly.
- **No incentive.** Offering anything for a review violates Google's terms and can get reviews stripped.

### If they ask what to write

> Honestly just what the job was and how it went. If you can mention it was a roof and that you're in [town], that's the part that helps us get found.

That is a fair thing to say. You are not telling them what opinion to hold.

## Getting the link

Google Business Profile → **Read reviews** → **Get more reviews** → copy the short link (`g.page/r/...`). Same link every time; save it to a text replacement.

## Timing

Best within a week of finishing a job, while it still feels recent. For past customers, tie it to something real — after a storm, or the first hard rain since the install.

## What to expect

Reviews influence local pack results within weeks, not days. Three or four roofing reviews from the right towns should start shifting the derived topics away from decks. Re-run the rank grid in about a month to see whether the dead zone around Martinsburg has moved.

## Don't

- Don't buy reviews or use a review-gating service that filters unhappy customers — both are grounds for Google stripping reviews or suspending the profile.
- Don't ask for reviews in bulk on one day. A sudden burst from a listing that averages one review every six weeks looks like what it is.
- Don't chase the deck customers again. They already reviewed, and more deck reviews push the topics further from roofing.
